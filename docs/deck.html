01 · The problem

30,000 pages. 50 review slots.

Editorial teams can deeply audit about 50 URLs per weekly sprint. The question isn't whether pages decay. It's which 50 a human should open first, before a Page-1 article slides off and takes its traffic with it.

02 · The trap

Random Forest looked superhuman, until it met new clients

A random split puts pages from the same client in both train and test. The trees learned each client's traffic scale, not how content decays. Holding out whole clients exposed it.

03 · The champion

Why monotonic weights won: 76% Precision@50

L2-regularized Logistic Regression gives every feature one direction of effect: older, less-visible pages carry more risk, whatever the client. That rule transfers to clients with a different traffic scale. Tree splits tuned to training-client scale don't.

05 · Governance

The model ranks. People decide.

Review authority scales with what a page stands to lose. The higher it ranks, the more senior the sign-off.

Review tier by SERP position · widths follow position ranges 1–5, 6–20, 21–30+
POSITION 1–5Tier 1 · High stakes

POSITION 6–20Tier 2 · Standard review

Senior Copywriter or Content Specialist.

POSITION 21+Tier 3 · Routine

Delegable to a junior editor or freelancer.

The no-go list · never automated
  • No unsupervised AI overwriteQueue output never pipes straight into an LLM that rewrites live pages.
  • No automated 301s or bulk deletionRemoving URLs can kill external backlinks that hold up domain authority.
  • No padding short utility pagesCalculators, FAQ, contact and policy pages are short on purpose.
  • No auto-swapped snippetsMeta tags follow brand and conversion intent, not keyword stuffing.
